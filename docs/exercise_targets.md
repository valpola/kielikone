# Exercise Targets — confusable words

Words that are individually correct in the deck but hard to **tell apart in use**.
Seeds for targeted contrastive drills (see the drill workflow in `CLAUDE.md`) —
**not** vocabulary fixes.

Captured from in-app notes; add more as they come in.

## Open

- **sinirlenmek** vs **kızmak** — *(note, issue #4)*. Same frame (both take dative
  `bir şeye`), so only the meaning separates them: `kızmak` = to get angry/cross at,
  `sinirlenmek` = to get irritated / worked up (`sinir` = nerve). **Not yet drilled.**

## Drilled — key findings to reinforce

- **beğenmek / hoşuna gitmek / sevinmek** — *(note, issue #13)*. Drilled 2026-07-30.
  The confusion dissolved once split into a *hard* part (case frames) and a *soft* part
  (meaning):

  | verb | subject | the liked / triggering thing | template |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | `beğenmek` | the person | **accusative `-i`** | `Filmi beğendim` |
  | `hoşuna gitmek` | **the thing** | nominative (bare); person = genitive + `hoş`+poss. | `Film hoşuma gitti` / `babamın hoşuna gitti` |
  | `sevinmek` | the person | **dative `-e`** | `Habere sevindim` |

  - **`sevinmek` is not a "like" verb at all** — it's "to be glad *that something
    happened*" (trigger = event/news). If the English can be reworded "I was happy
    that…", it's `sevinmek`.
  - It belongs to the **dative emotional-reaction family**, all taking `bir şeye`:
    `kızmak`, `sinirlenmek`, `üzülmek`, `şaşırmak`, `sevinmek`, `bayılmak`.
  - `beğenmek` vs `hoşuna gitmek` is **soft** — for most objects both are idiomatic and
    near-synonymous (`filmi beğendim` ≈ `film hoşuma gitti`). Nuance: `beğenmek` =
    judgment/approval (choosing, rating, appearance); `hoşuna gitmek` = the effect on you
    (taste, enjoyment). Don't agonize; do get the frame right.

  Status: dative frames produced correctly and unprompted (`bana`, `haberlere`, `sonuca`)
  — including the `sonuç → sonuca` softening. Re-test occasionally.
